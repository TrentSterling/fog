# Blind visual review, round 3 — reviewer B

## Scope

I inspected both anonymous contact sheets and all five full-size A–E images in the follow and grazing views. This round has its own mapping; letters below refer only to this round. I did not inspect keys, code, metadata, or other image directories. This is a static-image judgment; it does not establish animation stability or performance.

## Decision

**A narrowly leads C. Yes: A is a worthwhile visible improvement suitable to ship as an incremental visual update.** C is a very close alternative, and the difference between them is much smaller than their improvement over the smoothest candidate, D.

A adds visible internal breakup and small irregular folds while preserving the broad connected banks, open clearing, readable character, and exposed rocks. It avoids the conspicuous granular texture around the foreground rock in B. The extra structure is clearest in the follow view; the grazing view stays softer, but the banks still read as fog.

This is an incremental improvement, not a dramatic transformation into richly curled, filament-like ground fog. No external reference is included in this review, so I cannot certify a complete reference match. I also would not describe the remaining horizontal striations as completely eliminated.

## Ratings

Scale: 1 = poor; 5 = excellent. Organic detail rewards believable wisps/folds rather than texture quantity. Artifact restraint rewards absence of grain, horizontal bands, and repeated straight ribs.

## Ranking

**A ≈ C > E > B > D.**

A is my first pick, but confidence in the A-versus-C ordering is low. Both are in the same quality tier. A/C over D is a much clearer judgment. D ranks last for fit to this task, not because it is the ugliest image: it is clean, but retains the smoothness being addressed.

## What works in the leading pair

- The broad fog banks remain connected and substantial; the added variation does not fragment them into granular snow or isolated cotton balls.
- The central clearing remains open and legible in both views.
- The foreground fog remains translucent, and checkerboard detail shows through sparse edges.
- The character silhouette is immediately readable, including the legs.
- Rock tops remain exposed. Fog crossing the large foreground rock in the grazing view is substantially less conspicuously granular than B.
- Fine texture does not dominate the entire image. The viewer still sees large banks before seeing individual detail.

## Remaining imperfections

1. **The detail is shallow.** The strongest visible change is uneven interior density and small folds. There are still few clearly curled or stretched wisps with distinct local direction.
2. **The grazing view loses much of the structure.** The right-hand bank becomes a broad pale mass at the lower angle. This is the main reason the improvement should be called incremental.
3. **Faint horizontal striations remain.** They are easiest to find on the distant left-hand bank in the grazing view. They are restrained enough that I would not block this incremental update on the still images alone, but they are not entirely absent.
4. **Some front-edge wisps share a downward orientation.** They are soft rather than sharply ribbed, but a few portions of the near edge still suggest hanging streaks instead of irregular rolling fog.
5. **Dense areas remain uniformly pale.** The large right bank could eventually benefit from a little more meaningful curved structure or varied depth, while keeping the existing opacity and broad coverage.

## Ship assessment

Ship A as a modest visual improvement if the goal is to make the current smooth fog more organic while retaining its existing composition. C is also an acceptable choice if it has better motion stability or cost outside this image review. Do not oversell either candidate as a complete match to an unseen reference, or as eliminating all sampling artifacts. The visible gain is real, and the remaining imperfections are proportionate to an incremental update.

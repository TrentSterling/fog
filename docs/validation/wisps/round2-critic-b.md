# Blind visual review, round 2 — reviewer B

## Scope

I inspected the anonymous contact sheets and all A–G full-size images in both the follow and grazing views. I did not inspect the candidate key, code, metadata, or any other image set. The ratings below judge the visible images only; animation and performance are unassessed.

## Decision

**A is the best compromise. My final ship recommendation for the full requested improvement is no.** A has a visible but modest increase in internal variation over the smoother candidates while keeping the large banks and open clearing. However, its additional texture is still fairly shallow, and the grazing view reveals faint horizontal line texture. I would keep A as the best direction for the next iteration, rather than claim the organic wisp/fold brief is fully solved.

The set exposes a tradeoff: the stronger detail in C and F is easier to see, but it increasingly reads as grain and horizontal sampling bands. B and D are cleaner, but remain soft, pillowy masses. E is the cleanest and smoothest, with very little of the requested new detail. G adds conspicuous chunky streaks and repeated downward ribs.

## Ratings

Scale: 1 = poor; 5 = excellent. “Artifact restraint” rewards absence of conspicuous grain, bands, or repeated straight ribs. “Organic detail” rewards believable wisps and folds, not merely a higher amount of texture.

## Overall ranking

**A > B ≈ D > C > E > G > F.**

This is a ranking of the complete visual result against the brief, with obvious artifacts penalized. If judging texture amount alone, F and C would move upward, but that would reward the wrong thing. B versus D is a low-confidence distinction: their overall effect is extremely similar at native size.

## Why A leads

In the follow view, A gives the large rear and right-hand banks some interior breakup without destroying their continuity. The central checkerboard clearing remains obvious. The yellow character is immediately readable, and the exposed rock tops remain visible. Sparse foreground fog is still translucent, and A avoids G’s particularly strong straight hanging ribs.

A’s remaining weakness is that most of the extra variation feels like texture on a soft fog mass, rather than curled, stretched wisps with their own local direction. The grazing view also reveals faint horizontal striping across the far bank and around some denser patches. C and especially F amplify that same problem enough to be distracting.

## Remaining requirements for a confident ship

- Preserve A’s broad coverage, central clearing, readable character, and soft sparse edges.
- Add a small amount of irregular, medium-sized curved or stretched structure that can read as a fold at native image size.
- Avoid pushing the fine texture amplitude higher: C and F already show why this becomes grain and banding.
- Remove or sufficiently suppress the horizontal line texture visible at grazing angle before treating the improvement as finished.
- Keep the front/right bank from developing G’s aligned straight downward ribs.

I would not increase opacity to make the detail more visible. The current silhouette readability and exposed rocks are strengths across most of this set and should be retained.

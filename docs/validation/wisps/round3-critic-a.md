# Blind fog review — round 3, reviewer A

## Verdict

**Ranking: C ≳ A > E > B > D.** C and A are very close; C is my narrow preference, not a large or high-confidence separation.

**C and A pass as worthwhile visible improvements over the cleanest baseline, D.** Both add irregular internal folds and more varied wisps around the clearing while keeping the larger fog banks smooth and connected. Their foreground remains transparent, the rocks remain exposed, and the character is easy to read. The extra structure does not collapse into conspicuous repeated ribs, grainy cotton, or opaque foam.

**Proceed to motion and stress review with C, with A as an essentially equivalent alternate.** E is also an acceptable, more conservative improvement. B has a clearly inferior rock-edge fade and should not be chosen while the cleaner alternatives are available. D is clean but too plain for the requested detail improvement.

Only the assigned anonymous sheets and all ten 1280 × 720 originals were inspected. No keys, implementation, metadata, or other directories were inspected. Motion and temporal stability are not established by this review.

## Candidate findings

### C — preferred pass

**Follow view:** The right bank has visible irregular folds and pockets, rather than one uniformly blurred white surface. Around the character and clearing, thin patches alternate with stronger pieces in a varied pattern. The back-left bank retains a connected outline without becoming a comb of similarly angled ribs.

**Grazing view:** The detail quiets down appropriately with the flatter viewing angle. The foreground has diffuse wisps and clear gaps, while the large rock has a smooth partial immersion. The result reads as low mist rather than a fuzzy material spread over the ground.

**Artifacts:** Very faint parallel striation remains in some distant bank areas. It is minor at this resolution, and nothing comparable to B's foreground rock artifact dominates. There is no conspicuous granular coating of the floor.

**Decision:** Pass. The visible gain over D is worthwhile. Take this balance into motion testing rather than adding more global texture first.

### A — extremely close alternate

**Follow view:** The same successful balance of irregular folds, quiet smooth areas, and transparent gaps. The bank detail is visibly richer than D and somewhat stronger than E.

**Grazing view:** Keeps clearings and rock exposure intact. The bank remains continuous, with no strong repeated white strips. The larger rock fade is smooth enough to avoid drawing the eye.

**Artifacts:** A has a slight fine-textured appearance in some translucent foreground and distant areas. This is subtle, and my preference for C on restraint is small. I would not reject A over it or claim the two are dramatically different.

**Decision:** Pass, practically tied with C. If it performs or moves better, this would be a perfectly reasonable selection.

### E — conservative pass

**Follow view:** More shape and local density variation than D, but softer and less richly folded than A/C. Wisps at the clearing boundary are visible without becoming a repeated motif.

**Grazing view:** Restrained, transparent, and coherent. The large rock fade is clean. Most of the right bank becomes smooth, though enough broad variation survives to avoid looking completely featureless.

**Artifacts:** Faint distant line structure is still present, but no major grain or foreground stepping stands out.

**Decision:** Pass as a smaller improvement. Good fallback if the stronger variants reveal motion problems, but A/C better satisfy the request for visible added detail.

### B — loses on a conspicuous localized artifact

**Follow view:** The added folds and clearing-edge wisps are a real improvement over D. Character readability and bank coherence remain good.

**Grazing view:** The lower face and lower-right outline of the large foreground rock show obvious horizontal stepped/striped fragments in the fog fade. At the larger supplied resolution, this is unmistakable and catches the eye. The distant bank also has some fine parallel lines.

**Decision:** Do not select B for the next stage when A/C/E offer comparable or better useful detail with cleaner rock integration. Its entire fog shape is not a failure, but its artifact restraint is clearly worse.

### D — cleanest baseline, insufficient improvement

**Follow view:** Smooth, coherent fog banks and excellent scene readability. The large right mass has very little distinct internal wisp structure, and the foreground mostly consists of broad soft haze.

**Grazing view:** Clean transparency and rock exposure. The bank's broad shape remains plausible ground fog, but the close haze is comparatively uniform and the distant bank loses most smaller-scale character.

**Decision:** Useful clean reference. In isolation it is acceptable ground fog; relative to the user's requested extra wisps and detail, it is the weakest result.

## Is the softness acceptable?

**Yes for A/C, and conservatively for E.** Ground fog should retain soft transitions and smooth large masses. These candidates have enough irregular medium-scale folds and boundary wisps to keep that softness from becoming merely featureless blur. They do not need uniformly crisp filaments or a turbulent cloud surface to pass.

D demonstrates the point where this treatment becomes too plain for this specific improvement request. B demonstrates why extra variation is not worthwhile when a localized rendering artifact draws attention to itself.

## Motion review priorities

For C or A, check whether the thin wisps evolve continuously, whether the faint distant striation crawls, whether rock fades remain smooth as the camera moves, and whether the foreground stays transparent during movement. The stills already establish a suitable visual balance; further global sharpening or noise is not necessary before that test.

# Blind fog review — round 2, reviewer A

## Verdict

**Overall ranking: B ≈ D > A > E > C > G > F.**

**B and D pass as modest visual improvements.** They add irregular folds and wisps around the clearing while retaining smooth, continuous banks. The result still reads as low ground fog rather than a row of repeated strips or a granular cloud surface. I would accept this level of change on the supplied still-image evidence. It is not a dramatic fine-detail transformation, but it is a defensible improvement over the very smooth E.

I cannot reliably distinguish B from D at the supplied resolution in either view. Treat them as a visual tie, rather than interpreting the order of their labels as a preference.

A has more fine structure, but also more visible granularity. C and F push that grain too far. G pushes the larger forms toward chunky, similarly directed plumes. E remains the clean baseline but does not meet the added-detail goal as well as B/D.

Only the two assigned anonymous sheets and their fourteen A–G originals were inspected. No keys, source, metadata, or other directories were inspected. This review cannot assess motion or temporal stability.

## Close and distant observations

### B and D — accepted, tied

**Close:** The wisps beside and ahead of the character have varied widths and irregular openings. They look more like interconnected mist than a repeated set of diagonal blades. The foreground retains the checkerboard, and both the clearing and character are immediately legible. The large rock remains exposed with a plausible partially veiled base.

**Distant and grazing:** The left bank stays continuous without the conspicuous comb-like ribbing seen in rejected styles. The right bank has restrained density folds, with quiet smooth areas between them. The detail becomes subtle at grazing angle, which is acceptable for ground fog; forcing equal contrast everywhere would hurt the effect.

**Remaining flaw:** There is localized stepped/striped appearance in the fog fade over the lower-right edge of the large foreground rock at grazing angle. This is visible at native size, but does not dominate the scene or justify rejecting the entire improvement. It is the first artifact I would address in a later polish pass. The distant right bank also retains very faint parallel fine lines.

**Decision:** Pass as a measured improvement. Preserve the current balance rather than turning up texture globally.

### A — useful detail, beginning to look granular

**Close:** Soft foreground wisps and smooth transparent patches still work. Small speckled/dark patches become visible against the floor, particularly in the grazing view. These do not read as strong natural folds.

**Distant and grazing:** The bank has more small-scale surface structure than B/D. It is reasonably coherent, but the surface begins to resemble textured or fibrous material. The fine parallel structure becomes more apparent across the rear bank.

**Decision:** Close contender, but prefer B/D. A is not an unequivocal gain once the grazing view is included.

### E — clean, intentionally smooth baseline

**Close:** The cleanest transparent foreground and rock fade. The floor remains readable and the wisps do not distract from the character. Most of the fog variation is broad and blurred.

**Distant and grazing:** Smooth banks with gentle height changes. The right bank has few distinct internal wisps or fine folds. The low-frequency shapes are appropriate, but the image lacks the extra detail requested.

**Decision:** Keep as a fallback for maximum restraint. Prefer B/D for the requested improvement.

### C — fine texture crosses into grain

**Close:** Fine speckling is clearly visible over several foreground floor patches and around the rock base at grazing angle. The added detail looks increasingly like a rough texture overlay rather than thin mist.

**Distant and grazing:** The large shapes remain fairly coherent, but the surface has a persistent granular/fibrous appearance. Fine parallel lines also become easier to see. It is busier than A without a proportional improvement in natural wisps.

**Decision:** Reject as the selected improvement. Too much granular texture.

### G — large forms too chunky

**Close:** The wisps around the character are larger, thicker, and more directional. They form strong openings, but some of the white fingers resemble broad opaque brush strokes. The foreground rock fade has a distinct directional plume crossing it.

**Distant and grazing:** The right bank develops large, similarly directed lobes and columns. The left bank also shows a more repeated folded pattern. The effect has less convincing low, diffuse mist character than B/D.

**Decision:** Reject. Extra shape contrast is being spent on chunky plumes rather than delicate organic wisps.

### F — strongest rejection

**Close:** The floor and rock transition show conspicuous small-scale mottling and grain. Fine detail is distributed too widely, including quiet translucent regions.

**Distant and grazing:** Both banks look rough, fibrous, and somewhat cotton-like. Fine parallel line artifacts are visible across broad areas. The higher amount of texture does not create a more believable fog mass.

**Decision:** Reject. It is the busiest candidate and the least restrained.

## Recommendation

Use the B/D visual balance. It demonstrates that richer shape variation can coexist with intentionally smooth banks. Keep the transparent gaps and exposed rock tops. Any further work should be localized: reduce the stepped rock-edge fade and add only sparse, tapering small wisps where they help the bank boundary. Do not increase grain across the whole fog field to make a still frame look more detailed.

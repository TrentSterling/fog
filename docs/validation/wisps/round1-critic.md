# Blind fog review — round 1

## Verdict

**No candidate fully passes the requested target. Overall ranking: E, C, A, D, B.**

E is the best restrained starting point, with C close behind. Both preserve smooth, connected banks, a clear playable opening, exposed rock tops, and an immediately readable character. Neither provides enough convincing fine wisps or folded internal structure to count as a successful detailed-fog result. A and D add visible variation, but much of it reads as repeated slanted ribbons. B makes that defect unmistakable and adds conspicuous fine line artifacts.

This assessment used only the two anonymous sheets and the A–E originals in the assigned blind directories. All ten originals were inspected. No implementation, metadata, keys, or other output was inspected. These are still images, so temporal stability and motion are unassessed.

## Candidate findings

### E — best balance, incomplete detail

- **Close:** The foreground is transparent enough to retain the checkerboard and large rock silhouette. The light haze around the rock base gives a plausible partial immersion. There are some soft fingers around the clearing, but most transitions remain blurred rather than finely wispy.
- **Distant:** The continuous bank has gentle height and density changes, with somewhat more visible folding than C in the follow view. The right bank still tends toward a smooth white blanket, especially at grazing angle. Tiny far-edge striations are much less distracting than A/D/B.
- **Decision:** Keep as the visual baseline. Do not call this a finished improvement in fine detail. E's lead over C is modest.

### C — cleanest, too featureless

- **Close:** Smooth, believable transparency, excellent floor readability, and no distracting repeated foreground blades. The diffuse foreground haze becomes somewhat uniform.
- **Distant:** The left and right masses retain coherent shapes and clean silhouettes. Internal structure is very soft; much of the right bank appears like an undifferentiated dense layer.
- **Decision:** Strong fallback for clean rendering. Fails the specific request for more wispy, detailed fog.

### A — visible detail with an obvious repeating motif

- **Close:** More thin-looking variation appears in front of the character and across the foreground. Individual wisps are largely similarly angled, similarly sized slashes. Floor visibility is retained, but the pattern starts to read as translucent blades instead of turbulent mist.
- **Distant:** The left bank shows a regular run of slanted ribs. These are clearly visible in both cameras. The right bank has more mass variation than C, but remains fairly opaque and smooth between the rib-like features.
- **Decision:** Reject as the final result because the added detail is visibly repetitive. Better restrained than D or B, but less natural than E/C.

### D — too much ribbon structure

- **Close:** The foreground fingers have stronger gaps and darker separations than A. Their repeated direction makes the layer look shredded into ribbons. The character and rock tops remain clear, but the fog structure competes with the scene.
- **Distant:** Similar comb-like ribbing to A, with a stronger patterned surface. Fine parallel striations are also visible on the distant right bank in the grazing view.
- **Decision:** Reject. The extra contrast does not produce more convincing wisps, and the banks are less visually unified than A.

### B — decisive rejection

- **Close:** Large, similarly angled translucent strips dominate the foreground. At grazing angle, the strips cross the large rock and create abrupt dark separations. They are visually loud even though the character remains legible.
- **Distant:** The bank resembles a row of repeated white teeth or cotton strips. Fine horizontal/parallel line artifacts are conspicuous over much of the fog, particularly the right bank at grazing angle. The shape breaks into repeated elements instead of reading as a coherent smooth mass.
- **Decision:** Reject. Highest apparent texture activity, lowest naturalism. It violates both the repeated-streak and artifact-restraint requirements.

## What the next visual candidate must resolve

Preserve E/C's soft connected mass, clearings, and transparency. Add irregular folds and tapering wisps that vary in direction, width, spacing, and strength. The added detail should be sparse enough to leave quiet smooth areas, and should remain natural on the far left bank and at grazing angle. Avoid turning the whole bank into similarly slanted ribbing or making the right bank a more opaque white surface.

The immediate failure is **organized repetition**, not a lack of raw high-frequency contrast. More detail of the A/D/B kind would move the image farther from the goal.
